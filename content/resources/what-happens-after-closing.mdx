## Executive overview

Closing transfers agreed rights and obligations. It does not automatically complete the operational handoff. Funds must move correctly, authority and access must change, employees and customers need accurate communication, and seller obligations may continue through transition support, representations, earnouts, indemnities, or a seller note.

The definitive agreements and closing documents control. Use this guide as an operating checklist, not an interpretation of legal obligations.

## Confirm closing conditions

Before closing, maintain a condition tracker showing the requirement, responsible party, evidence, status, deadline, and approval. Conditions can include financing, consents, payoff letters, leases, licenses, insurance, entity authority, employment arrangements, working-capital schedules, and transaction documents.

Do not announce completion or transfer control until the authorized closing team confirms the conditions and effective time.

## Review the funds flow

The funds-flow statement should identify purchase funds, debt payoff, taxes or withholdings where applicable, expenses, escrow or holdback, seller proceeds, and other transfers. Verify wiring instructions through a known, independent channel to reduce fraud risk.

The seller should understand which amounts are:

- Cash at closing
- Escrowed or held back
- Paid to creditors or advisors
- Deferred under a seller note
- Contingent under an earnout
- Retained as equity
- Subject to later working-capital adjustment

Headline price is not the same as net proceeds or immediately available cash.

## Transfer ownership and authority

Complete the legal and operational control matrix. Address governance records, banking, payroll, contracts, insurance, domains, software, accounting, payment processors, keys, facilities, phone systems, social accounts, and records.

Use least privilege and a verified handoff. Disable access that should end; do not share personal credentials. Preserve required records and logs. Some changes depend on third-party approval or processing and should remain tracked until confirmed.

## Communicate with employees, customers, and vendors

Execute the approved day-one plan. Keep facts consistent and identify where questions go. Avoid promises that are not in the parties’ authority.

Employees need clarity on reporting, payroll, benefits, leadership, policies, and immediate priorities. Customers need service continuity, contacts, billing, and timing. Vendors may need updated authority, credit, tax forms, or payment instructions.

Read [Preparing Employees and Customers for a Transition](/resources/preparing-employees-and-customers).

## Manage seller transition support

The seller’s role should be written, time-limited, and measurable. Define hours, availability, location, responsibilities, authority, customer introductions, compensation, expenses, confidentiality, and escalation.

Use a knowledge-transfer log with topic, recipient, evidence, open issue, and completion. Avoid allowing the seller to retain shadow control after authority has moved.

## Monitor working capital

If the transaction includes a working-capital adjustment, follow the agreement’s definitions, accounting principles, measurement date, preparation process, review rights, and dispute procedure. Do not improvise the calculation from a generic template.

Operationally, monitor cash, receivables, payables, inventory, deferred revenue, payroll, and near-term capital needs. A dispute over the closing balance should not distract from keeping the business stable.

## Track representations and continuing obligations

The agreements may contain representations, covenants, indemnities, escrows, survival periods, notices, insurance duties, cooperation, noncompetition or nonsolicitation terms where lawful, and record-access obligations.

Create a confidential obligations calendar reviewed by counsel. Record responsible party, deadline, evidence, notice method, and completion. Do not rely on memory.

## Administer earnouts

Earnouts depend on defined metrics, periods, accounting rules, operating covenants, information rights, and dispute processes. Sellers and buyers should understand what behavior is permitted and how results are calculated.

Maintain the records specified in the agreement. Separate operational reporting from the contractual calculation, and route disagreements through the agreed process. General guidance cannot determine the correct result.

## Administer the seller note

Record principal, interest, payments, maturity, reporting, security, guarantees, subordination, insurance, and default provisions. Assign responsibility for servicing and statements.

Read [Seller Financing](/resources/seller-financing). A change in payment, waiver, or enforcement action needs professional review and documented authority.

## Handle post-close disputes

Use the notice and dispute provisions in the transaction documents. Preserve evidence, limit communications to authorized participants, and avoid self-help actions that may violate agreements or damage the business.

Create an issue record with facts, source documents, dates, financial effect, owner, required notice, and professional advice. Distinguish an operating problem from a contractual claim.

## Protect records and confidentiality

Closing can change who owns business records while leaving the seller with tax, indemnity, note, or cooperation needs. Follow the agreement’s retention and access provisions. Keep only authorized copies, protect personal and confidential information, and use the required request process.

Create a records map showing owner, location, retention period, access authority, legal hold, and destruction process. Remove seller access to systems when it is no longer required, even if the relationship remains friendly.

Public communication should match approved facts. Do not disclose purchase terms, customer information, employee details, disputes, or financing arrangements unless authorized.

## Run a transition command meeting

Use one recurring meeting for the first weeks after closing. Include only responsible participants. Review:

- Safety, service, and customer issues
- Cash, billing, payroll, and working capital
- Employee questions and departures
- Vendor, landlord, lender, and regulator dependencies
- Access and system migrations
- Seller support milestones
- Agreement obligations and deadlines
- Decisions, owners, and due dates

Keep operational work separate from privileged legal discussions where counsel advises. Close completed items and escalate blockers quickly.

## Define completion

The transition is not complete because the calendar reached day 100. Define completion criteria: authority transferred, critical access changed, customer and employee handoffs stable, seller knowledge delivered, required consents obtained, obligations calendared, open disputes assigned, and normal operating governance established.

Some financial obligations may continue for years. Move them from the transition tracker into a controlled administration process with named owners and professional review.

## The first 30 days

1. Confirm every access and authority transfer.
2. Complete employee, customer, and vendor follow-up.
3. Stabilize cash, payroll, billing, and service.
4. Review open jobs, commitments, and disputes.
5. Complete priority knowledge transfers.
6. Calendar continuing obligations.
7. Establish seller-note or earnout administration.
8. Hold weekly transition reviews.

## The first 100 days

1. Measure customer retention, employee turnover, quality, backlog, and cash.
2. Resolve remaining third-party consents and system migrations.
3. Complete the seller’s defined support milestones.
4. Review working-capital or other post-close calculations.
5. Close knowledge gaps and update procedures.
6. Confirm governance, reporting, and strategic priorities.
7. Transition from transaction management to normal operating cadence.

## The seller’s next chapter

The seller may need time to adjust after years of identity, relationships, and routine tied to the business. Build a personal plan for liquidity, taxes, family, health, purpose, confidentiality, and future involvement with qualified professionals.

Respect the new owner’s authority. Follow continuing agreements, direct business questions through the agreed channel, and avoid informal commitments on behalf of the company.

Before leaving the transition cadence, the seller should confirm who administers every continuing payment, notice, tax cooperation request, retained record, and professional relationship. A clean operational exit does not cancel written obligations.

Document that confirmation.

Give each obligation a backup owner.

## Educational boundary
